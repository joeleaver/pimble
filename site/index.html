One place for everything you write down, and it's actually yours.

Pimble is a personal information manager: notes, plans, references, whatever else you'd otherwise scatter across a dozen apps, organized into one tree you shape yourself. It lives in a folder on your own disk, so it's there whether or not you have a network, and whether or not Pimble's servers are running. Share a piece of it, and edits from two people — or two of your own devices — merge back together on their own.

Organized your way

A store is a folder on disk, and everything in it is a node in a tree: a page of formatted text, a project, a room in the house, a person you keep notes about — whatever shape your life actually takes. There's no separate database quietly keeping itself in sync with your files. The folder is the store. And because it's a folder you own, it's still there even if Pimble the company someday isn't.

Search that understands what you meant

Every store keeps its own index, right there next to your files: ordinary keyword search across every title and every word you've written, narrowing results as you type. Alongside it, semantic search finds a passage by what it's about, even when you don't remember the words you used for it — and because the model behind it runs locally on your machine, that search never touches a network either.

No more "someone else has this open"

Two people can open the same page at the same time — on two laptops anywhere in the world, or two windows on your own machine — and both keep working. Nothing locks, nothing gets thrown away, and neither person waits for the other to close their editor. It's the same trick that lets Pimble work offline in the first place: every document is built to reconcile changes from more than one place, whether that place has been disconnected for five minutes or five months.

Under the hood, for the curious:

  • Nodes in a tree

    A store is a folder of nodes, arranged however you like. There's no separate database to keep in sync with the files — the folder is the store.

  • CRDTs, not locks

    A node's content and the tree's structure are both CRDTs: data structures built to merge. Two edits from two places reconcile automatically, and neither one is thrown away.

  • Mounts

    Any subtree of any store can appear inside any other store's tree, resolved to the same underlying node wherever it shows up — whether that store is on this machine or another server entirely.

  • A local search index

    Each store keeps its own index on disk: keyword search over titles and text, plus a small embedding model for semantic search, both running without a network call.
